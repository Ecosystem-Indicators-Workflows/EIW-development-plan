4  User Requirements for Ecosystem Indicator Workflows

4.1 Overview

Workshop participants explored a series of realistic workflow development scenarios and identified user needs, technical challenges, and priorities for future Ecosystem Indicator Workflows (EIWs).

Workshop participants consistently emphasised that the value of Ecosystem Indicator Workflows extends beyond indicator calculation. Users require workflows that are adaptable, reproducible, transparent, well-documented, and capable of integrating diverse ecosystem datasets while remaining accessible to users with varying technical capabilities.

4.2 Considerations for user requirements

4.2.1 Workflow Adaptability

Users require workflows that can be adapted to different ecosystem types, geographic regions, and assessment purposes without requiring complete redevelopment. Stakeholders identified adaptability as one of the most important requirements for the initiative.

Participants repeatedly highlighted that existing workflows are often difficult to repurpose because ecosystem-specific assumptions and methods are embedded throughout the workflow rather than isolated in configurable components.

4.2.2 Reproducibility and Transparency

Users require workflows that can be independently reproduced, verified, and maintained over time. Reproducibility was identified as a core requirement underpinning scientific credibility and long-term adoption.

Users highlighted challenges associated with legacy workflows, undocumented dependencies, restricted data access, and software version incompatibilities. Participants identified containerisation and explicit environment management as important mechanisms for maintaining reproducibility.

4.2.3 Data Discovery, Access and Integration

Users require workflows that simplify the process of locating, understanding, accessing, and combining ecosystem and indicator datasets from multiple sources. Data preparation was consistently identified as the most time-consuming component of indicator development.

Participants highlighted common challenges including fragmented datasets, inconsistent survey methodologies, varying spatial resolutions, uneven geographic coverage, and difficulties obtaining access to restricted datasets.

4.2.4 Classification and Vocabulary Alignment

Users require ecosystem workflows that support alignment between national ecosystem classifications and international standards.

Stakeholders identified ecosystem classification mapping as one of the largest barriers to workflow development and noted that a substantial proportion of effort is currently spent reconciling classification systems across jurisdictions and assessment frameworks.

4.2.5 Documentation, Training and Knowledge Transfer

Users require documentation and training resources that accommodate varying levels of technical expertise.

Participants consistently identified documentation and training as critical enablers of workflow adoption. Requirements varied across user groups, but all groups emphasised the need for high-quality documentation and practical examples.

4.2.6 Accessibility and Usability

Users require workflows that minimise technical barriers to adoption while supporting a diverse user community.

Workshop participants demonstrated strong familiarity with R, Python, RStudio, QGIS, and Jupyter-based environments, while familiarity with specialised platforms and workflow technologies varied considerably. As a result, workflow design should prioritise accessibility and user experience over technical complexity where practical.

4.2.7 Validation, Uncertainty and Scientific Confidence

Users require workflows that support the transparent evaluation of indicator quality and reliability.

Participants noted that uncertainty information is frequently omitted from final products despite being critical for interpretation and decision-making. Validation approaches and integration of expert judgement were identified as areas requiring greater consistency across workflows.

4.3 Priority User Requirements

Across all workshops, the following requirements emerged as the highest priorities for the Ecosystem Indicator Workflow program:

  1. Adaptable and modular workflow architecture.
  2. Reproducible workflows supported by version-controlled computational environments.
  3. Alignment with the IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology and other recognised standards.
  4. Transparent data provenance and metadata.
  5. Guidance for integrating heterogeneous ecosystem datasets.
  6. Comprehensive documentation and tiered training materials.
  7. Explicit treatment of validation, uncertainty, and expert judgement.
  8. FAIR-compliant workflows, tools, metadata, and outputs.

Requirements 1 to 7 provide the foundation for prioritising workflow functionality and defining the staged delivery roadmap described in subsequent sections of this document. Requirement 8 is embedded in all activities of the project.

4.4 Key requirements

The following list of key requirements represent a comprehensive set of needs identified across stakeholder groups. They map to the priority requirements, except that the points 5 and 6 are merged for practical reasons.

ID Description
AD Adaptability
AD01 Modular workflow designs that clearly separate ecosystem-specific components from generic analytical components.
AD02 Clearly documented points within workflows where ecosystem definitions, data sources, indicator variables, and assessment purposes can be substituted.
AD03 The ability to apply a common workflow structure across multiple ecosystem types.
AD04 The ability to support alternative assessment frameworks, including ecosystem risk, ecosystem condition, recovery, and ecosystem accounting applications.
AD05 Guidance for reconciling global datasets with local and jurisdictional data sources.
RE Reproducibility and transparency
RE01 Fully documented workflow processes from data acquisition through to final outputs.
RE02 Clear provenance records for all input datasets.
RE03 Version-controlled workflow code and analytical methods.
RE04 Documented software dependencies and computational environments.
RE05 Long-term maintainability as software platforms and packages evolve.
RE06 Repeatable execution across multiple computing environments.
VO Classification and vocabularies
VO01 Alignment with the IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology.
VO02 Clear mapping between local ecosystem classifications and global typologies.
VO03 Use of authoritative identifiers where possible.
VO04 Machine-readable classification mappings and metadata.
VO05 Transparent documentation of classification decisions and assumptions.
DD Data discovery
DD01 Guidance on identifying authoritative ecosystem and indicator datasets.
DD02 Clear documentation of data provenance, quality, currency, and fitness-for-purpose.
DD03 Consistent approaches for harmonising heterogeneous datasets.
DD04 Mechanisms to support integration of biological surveys, monitoring data, remote sensing products, and spatial datasets.
DD05 Support for workflows operating across local, cloud, and high-performance computing environments.
DD06 Explicit documentation of data limitations and known biases.
DO Documentation and training
DO01 Plain-language workflow documentation.
DO02 Worked examples using real ecosystem assessments.
DO03 Step-by-step implementation guidance.
DO04 Technical documentation describing workflow functions and analytical methods.
DO05 Guidance on interpretation of outputs, assumptions, limitations, and uncertainty.
DO06 Training resources suitable for beginner, intermediate, and advanced users.
AC Accessibility
AC01 Support for commonly used open-source tools and platforms.
AC02 Use of technologies already familiar to the target user community where possible.
AC03 Clear installation and setup instructions.
AC04 Support for users operating within institutional security and software restrictions.
AC05 Outputs that can be interpreted by both technical and non-technical users.
VA Validation
VA01 Explicit validation procedures.
VA02 Documentation of assumptions and methodological limitations.
VA03 Quantification and communication of uncertainty.
VA04 Support for expert judgement where empirical validation data are unavailable.
VA05 Consistent reporting of confidence in indicator outputs.
